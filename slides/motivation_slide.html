Grounding models in geometry · Scale & Simplicity
Why GRaM Data is inherently geometric. Real-world data originates from physical systems governed by geometric and physical laws — yet most ML methods treat it as plain vectors.

Geometry offers guiding principles — from group equivariance to non-Euclidean metrics — that preserve the symmetries and structure inherent in data, enabling better, more interpretable representations and generative models.

Our guiding principle
An approach is geometrically grounded if it respects the geometric structure of the problem domain and supports geometric reasoning.

Preserving data geometry Symmetries & structure

Respect the structure that is already there.

Preservation of symmetries e.g., equivariant operators and layers.
Geometric representation systems e.g., Clifford algebras, steerable vectors with Clebsch–Gordan products.
Isometric latent mappings e.g., latent representations via pullback metrics.

Inducing geometric structure Priors & embeddings

Shape models with geometric inductive biases.

Geometric priors e.g., curvature, symmetry, or topological constraints via explicit regularization.
Non-Euclidean generative models e.g., diffusion or flow matching on domains with a predefined metric.
Metric-preserving embeddings e.g., latent spaces where geodesic distances map to Euclidean ones.

Geometry in theoretical analysis Tools for understanding

Geometry as a lens for deep learning theory.

Data & latent geometry Insights on the data manifold, statistical manifold, or latent variables.
Loss landscape geometry Curvature, critical points, and generalization along optimization trajectories.
Theoretical frameworks Differential geometry, algebraic geometry, group theory for generation & representation.
Open problems Unresolved questions at the intersection of geometry and learning.
